Play on paper

Print it, fold it, take it somewhere without a screen. A pencil and one die is the whole kit.

  • Page one explains everything, so you can hand the pack to someone who has never played.
  • One hole to a page, drawn big enough to scribble your shots straight onto.
  • Every sheet carries its course code, so you can bring the same eighteen holes back to the screen later.

How to play

The idea. A ball ○, a cup ●, and a page of dots in between. Par is 6 everywhere. Fewest strokes at the end wins the argument.

Dice golf. The die decides how far; you decide where. Roll it, add a dot if you are standing on fairway, lose one if you are stuck in sand, then fire along any of the eight lines. The ball travels straight and stops where it stops.

Speed golf. No die at all — pick a club and live with it. Driver goes 6, but only off the fairway, and it clears trees. Iron goes 3, or 2 out of sand, and clears nothing. Putter goes 1, always, forever.

The ground. Trees are solid: you cannot land on one and you cannot shoot through one — unless you are swinging from the fairway, in which case you sail clean over the top. Water you may fly; you may not finish in it. Sand quietly takes a dot off your next swing. Hills are little squares of arrows, 2×2 up to 4×4: solid heads are a mound and shove the ball a dot away from the middle, open heads are a hollow and draw it a dot inward. Land on another arrow and you keep going.

Sinking it. Land on the cup and it drops. Sail over it and finish exactly one dot past and it drops as well — call it rattling in off the back. Two dots past is just two dots past.

Second chances. Every hole hands you one free re-roll off the tee. After that you are down to six mulligans for the entire round, so spend them like they are yours. You may also ignore the die entirely and putt a single dot instead — frequently the sensible move, rarely the chosen one.

Playing together. Everyone tees off in turn, then whoever lies furthest from the cup plays next. Keep your eyes open: roughly one notebook in three has a bigfoot loitering somewhere on it. Find him, click him, pocket a mulligan, say nothing.
